Opus reading every chunk fixes 94% of transcription errors. Glossaries fix about half.

We tested four ways to repair Whisper mistakes in the Medical Medium transcripts, each with Haiku, Sonnet and Opus. Fable 5.1, which was not a contestant, wrote the answer key: 219 confirmed errors across 150 sample chunks.

Recommendation

Run Opus over every chunk, with an Opus-written glossary in front

  1. Opus writes the glossary once from the corpus's rare words, for about $6. Its glossary was the only one with zero wrong fixes in the sample, and 149 of 149 sampled corpus-wide replacements were right.
  2. Opus then reads every chunk and returns small edits, never rewritten text. This is the only setup that catches ordinary words misheard in context, which are most of the errors.
  3. Edits are stored as a reviewable list (file, cue, from, to). Repeated term fixes move into the glossary, so later updates need fewer model calls.

Cost for the full 12.5M words is about $250 with batch pricing, or roughly 14 hours on the Claude subscription through the CLI. Opus alone without the glossary scores the same (F1 .951 vs .949); the glossary is there for consistency across the corpus, not for accuracy.

Where one approach beats another

Ordinary words misheard in context decide the result

These are real English words, so nothing flags them as unusual. Only a model reading the sentence notices. Opus caught 95% of them; Haiku 60%, Sonnet 70%, every glossary 0%.

got rid of fat thatit's all been flooding floating around

One "known" glossary entry was wrong everywhere

The earlier scan listed "monolating" as monolaurin. In all 23 corpus hits it is the Mono Eating Cleanse. The static glossary broke every one and inflated monolaurin search hits from 11 to 31 chunks. Opus's glossary review caught it; Haiku's kept the wrong fix.

the monolating mono eating cleanse

Names come in more spellings than any list holds

Vimergy alone appears in dozens of forms. Mechanical mining even mapped one misspelling onto another. Models recognise the brand from context.

Cheaper models over-edit or guess the wrong fix

Sonnet changed correct text 25 times. Haiku often picked a plausible but wrong term. Opus agreed with itself on 72% of edits across two runs, against about 55% for the others.

Sonnet: radical rancid fatsHaiku: mercer Mercury (was MRSA)Haiku: problins probiotics (was propolis)

Where the glossary is better: the same answer every time

A glossary makes identical edits on every run and can be read and corrected by hand. The Opus glossary made 6,558 corpus edits with no judged errors and lifted "Vimergy" search hits from 1,364 to 2,146 chunks.

How much to trust this

  • The sample is small. Only 26 confirmed errors fall in the random chunks, so score gaps under about .03 are noise. Opus alone and Opus with a glossary are a tie.
  • All four models come from one family. Graded by Opus's own answer key, Opus scored .993; graded by Fable, .951. Fable may share some of Opus's blind spots.
  • Scores are not yet adjusted for your answers above. Some answer-key items are arguably out of scope, like "golden seal" to "goldenseal".
  • Costs come from CLI runs, which add about 40% overhead tokens. A direct API run with caching would cost roughly 0.6× these figures.
